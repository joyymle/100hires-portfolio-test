## Core writing framework: Step 0 → Step 5

### Step 0 — Check search intent (before any tool)
**30-second Google gut check:**
- All how-to guides on page 1 → write a how-to
- All listicles → write a listicle
- All product pages → a blog post **probably won't rank** — pick a different keyword

Google has already decided the format searchers want. **Match it and execute better** — don't fight intent. Skipping this step makes all later optimization useless.

### Step 1 — Research and create outline
**Surfer Content Editor tabs:** Competitors | Questions | Notes

| Action | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Competitor analysis** | Expand top-ranking outlines — headings, structure, topics covered |
| **AI outline** | Surfer generates outline from what's working; hit **Insert Outline** as starting point |
| **70/30 rule** | **~70% proven topics** all competitors cover (table stakes) + **~30% unique angles** (gaps only 1–2 mention, or none cover but you know are relevant) |
| **Gap research with AI** | Paste outline into Claude; ask what's missing for search intent; deep research across Reddit, YouTube, Facebook, LinkedIn for fresh user signals |
| **Concrete examples** | Use agents to hunt real-world examples (e.g. standout case studies) — move reader from theory to proof |
| **Brand context (optional)** | Add brand voice, product details, non-negotiable talking points before AI draft — saves as reusable template |

**Example gaps surfaced for "how to write a case study":** client permission/buy-in, interview questions for stakeholders, video case studies, anonymized data when clients won't be named.

### Step 2 — Write and optimize
- Write **heading by heading** — strong outline = "paint by numbers"; reframes 3,500 words as one section at a time
- **Hook philosophy:** write hook first *or* after halfway through — whichever unlocks the angle (example hook: *"Nobody actually reads case studies, but you need them anyway — here's how to make one worth skimming."*)
- Real-time feedback via sidebar steps + dual optimization tracks (see Content Score section below)
- **On-page targets:** word count, headings, paragraphs modeled on top rankers — **compass, not hard rules**

### Step 3 — Internal linking (~5 minutes, high leverage)
**Contextual links** (body copy, Wikipedia-style), not just nav/footer:

| Direction | Target |
|-----------|--------|
| **Outgoing** | 3–5 links from new post → existing related content |
| **Incoming** | 3–5 existing posts → link **to** new post (easy to forget; tells Google the page matters) |

Connect GSC + Surfer for semantic (not just keyword-match) suggestions. Builds topical context: e.g. keyword research guide ↔ case study post = site covers content marketing comprehensively.

### Step 4 — Pre-publish and review (quality gate)
**Human polish:**
- Reread **out loud on a walk** — catches awkward phrasing, hedging, throat-clearing
- Ask: screenshots, expert quotes, custom visuals (infographics/diagrams), embedded YouTube videos (any relevant video — breaks up copy, boosts engagement)

**Surfer QC tools:**

| Tool | Purpose |
|------|---------|
| **Readability score** | Target broad accessibility — simplify if "college level" when you want mass reach |
| **Plagiarism checker** | Original content ranks; critical if AI helped draft (AI can mirror published passages) |
| **AI readability** | Scans formatting AI prefers when citing: clear structure, defined terms, factual claims with context — *"Would an AI model pull a clean accurate answer from this page?"* |

**Pre-flight checklist:** readable for humans + AI ✓ | no plagiarism ✓ | every section delivers on its heading ✓

### Step 5 — Publish or export
- **Meta tags:** keyword near front of title; description tells searcher exactly what they'll get
- **Export:** WordPress direct publish, formatted text, markdown, or HTML
- **Timeline:** don't expect week-one results — **3–6 months** typical; faster in low competition, longer in legal/finance/health/digital marketing
- **Post-publish:** check GSC at **30 / 60 / 90 days**; if stuck, return to Content Editor — fresh SER data updates Content Score so you can see where you've fallen behind

## Content Score: SEO vs. AI Search Score

Surfer rebuilt the score around **SERP reverse-engineering** — we don't know Google's algorithm; we observe what ranks and match patterns.

| Score | What it measures | How to read it |
|-------|------------------|----------------|
| **Overall Content Score** (center, /100) | Combined readiness | How closely your content matches winning SER patterns (entities, facts, word count, structure) |
| **SEO Score** (left) | Google ranking readiness | Topical coverage vs. pages Google already rewards |
| **AI Search Score** (right) | LLM citation readiness | Likelihood your page becomes the source when ChatGPT, Perplexity, or Google AI Mode answers queries in your topic |

**Goal:** push **both into green** — organic traffic from Google today + citations in AI answers tomorrow. Every AI Search Score point up = better shot at being the page AI names with your brand inside the answer.

## Dual optimization tracks (sidebar)

### Optimize for SEO — **Entities**
- **Entity** = specific concept/term top rankers consistently mention (topic vocabulary)
- Example: "customer testimonials," "ROI," "pain points" for case-study content
- Display: e.g. **38/81** covered — don't need all 81; cover relevant ones **naturally**
- **Not keyword stuffing** — skip forced or unnatural terms; missing **core concepts** = topical holes

### Optimize for AI Search — **Facts**
- **Facts** = concrete, verifiable ideas LLMs historically cite for queries around your topic
- Surfer pulls from **two source types:** AI models (ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews, Perplexity) + top-ranking web pages
- Grouped by topic (e.g. "Writing tips": identify your goal, include specific data, make customer the protagonist)
- Logic: AI answers from pages with **real specific factual information**, not vague advice
- **Auto Optimize:** one-click adds missing entities + facts while preserving voice

**If score stuck after Auto Optimize:** gap is usually **coverage**, not surface entities/facts — reopen Competitors tab, find H2/H3 **three rankers share** that your outline skipped; write that section (**8/10 times** fixes the gap).

## Actionable content fundamentals

### 1. BLUF — Bottom line upfront
Lead with the answer. Search *"how long does it take to rank on Google?"* → first sentence: **"Typically 3–6 months."** Then earn depth with nuance. Don't bury the lead.

### 2. Short paragraphs
**2–3 sentences max.** Walls of text kill mobile readability; most traffic is mobile.

### 3. Subheadings every 200–300 words
Help readers scan; give Google and AI **explicit section signals**. Phrase headings as **questions users actually ask** — works for traditional and AI SEO.

### 4. Clarity over cleverness
Write at **~6th grade reading level** (max ~8th). No sentence should require a reread.

### 5. Firsthand experience on top of AI drafts
Surfer AI draft (~90% there with brand knowledge + live SER analysis) still needs **personal experience** layered in — authority + uniqueness AI alone can't fake.

### 6. Principles that transcend the tool
Cover topic with **authority**, show **firsthand experience** where you have it, **match search intent** — true regardless of stack.

## Caveats worth noting

- **Surfer product walkthrough** — Content Editor, AI drafts, Auto Optimize, GSC linking are promotional but principles stand alone.
- Content Score is **SERP pattern matching**, not a Google ranking guarantee.
- **AI Search Score** predicts citation *potential* — doesn't replace Brand Radar / AI Tracker for mention monitoring.
- Timeline claims (3–6 months) vary heavily by niche and competition.
- AI draft + Auto Optimize require **human review** for E-E-A-T, accuracy, and plagiarism risk.
